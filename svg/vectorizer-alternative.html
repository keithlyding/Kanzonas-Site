Why people trace pictures here

Your picture stays with you

Tracing runs on your computer. Nothing is uploaded, so client logos and private drawings never leave it.

Picks the settings

Auto settings chooses the colors (a logo's exact ones, up to 64), smoothing, the background, and thin lines for drawings and plans. Change anything and the SVG updates.

Exact colors, sharp edges

Flat colors come out exactly as in the picture, with no rings of in-between shades, so the SVG is ready for cutting machines, vinyl and printing.

Crop and background

Trace only part of a picture, and make its background see-through.

Many files at once

A command line converts one picture or a whole folder, with the same settings or picked for each.

Free, really

No account, no credits, no trial and no watermark. Free and open source (GPL).

Where KanzonasSVG is different

Honestly, so you know what to expect:

  • It traces; it doesn't edit. Open the SVG in Inkscape, Illustrator or another vector editor to change it.
  • No Mac version, and no web version.
  • Not AI-based: it's Inkscape's tracing (Potrace and Autotrace), fixed and tuned. Photos turn into many flat shapes, as with any tracer.

Questions

Is it good for Cricut, Silhouette and laser cutting?

Logos and flat artwork are what it does best: exact colors and clean edges, with the background removed if you like. Save the SVG and open it in your machine's software.

Is it really free?

Yes: free and open source (GPL), with no account, credits, watermark or paid version.

Does it go online?

Only once a day to check whether a newer version exists (you can turn that off). Your pictures are never sent anywhere.
