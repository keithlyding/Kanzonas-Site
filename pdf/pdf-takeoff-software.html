Free PDF takeoff software

For estimators and quantity surveyors: do quantity takeoffs on PDF drawings for free: set the scale, measure lengths, areas, perimeters and volumes, count items by group, and export the totals to CSV for your spreadsheet. KanzonasPDF is free and open source, for Windows, Linux and Mac, with no account and no subscription.

Takeoff tools, free

Scale every sheet

Pick a preset (1/4" = 1'-0", 1" = 20', 1:100...), calibrate on a known dimension, or let it read the scale written in the title block, for one sheet or a whole set at once. The page's scale is always in sight, and a red "Scale not set" warns you before you measure.

Measure

Length, polylength, area with perimeter, perimeter, volume (area × depth), angle and radius, with snapping. Change the scale or drag a point and the values update.

Count by group

Named counts ("Smoke detectors", "Data outlets", "Duplex receptacles"), each in its own color and layer, numbered 1, 2, 3... Delete one and the rest renumber; a gap or a number used twice is pointed out.

Room by room

Draw named spaces (Room 101, Level 2, Zone A) and filter the markups list by space: quantities per room, per floor or per area of work.

Totals and export

The measurement summary totals every length, area, volume and count, and exports to CSV for Excel or your estimating spreadsheet. Legends on the sheet list each count and its total.

Revisions

Compare two revisions of a sheet with every change clouded, then replace the sheet with the new revision and keep your takeoff markups on it.

A takeoff in five steps

  1. Open the drawing set (or a folder of sheets, browsed by sheet number and title).
  2. Measure > Read scales from title blocks, then check one known dimension and calibrate if a sheet was printed at reduced size.
  3. Measure runs, areas and volumes, and click each item with Count, one group per kind of item.
  4. Place a legend on the sheet to show each group and its total.
  5. Export the measurement summary to CSV and price it in your spreadsheet.

Everything is saved as standard PDF markups, so the marked-up set opens in Acrobat, Bluebeam and other PDF viewers too.

What it doesn't do

Honestly, so you know before you rely on it:

  • It measures and counts; it doesn't price. No cost database, assemblies or bid forms: export the quantities to your spreadsheet or estimating program.
  • Counting is by clicking each item; it doesn't find symbols on the sheet by itself.
  • No cloud: no shared online sessions. Share the marked-up PDF the way you already do.
  • No iPad or phone app, and on a Mac only Apple silicon (M1 or newer).
  • A young project: try it on a copy of a real job before you rely on it, and tell us what's missing. Most of its features came from requests.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes: free and open source (AGPL), for personal and commercial work, with no watermarks, no trial period and no paid version. No account, and nothing is uploaded.

Does it work in metric?

Yes. Metric scales (1:50, 1:100, 1:200...) and imperial ones (1/4" = 1'-0", 1" = 20'...) work the same way, and measurements are in the unit of the page's scale, metric or feet and inches.

Can it replace Bluebeam for takeoffs?

For measuring and counting on PDFs, for many people, yes; see KanzonasPDF as a free Bluebeam Revu alternative for a side-by-side comparison and the differences.

Can it handle big drawing sets?

That's what it's built for. Every build checks that a sheet of 60,000 lines opens and draws in under 5 seconds, and that zooming stays fast.

Does it go online?

Only to check whether a newer version exists (you can turn that off), and to reach a timestamp server if you add a trusted timestamp to a digital signature.
